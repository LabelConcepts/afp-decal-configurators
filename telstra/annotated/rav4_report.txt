METHOD NOTE ON D1 vs P1 (driver vs passenger side identification)
p2's top-left profile photo is EXPLICITLY labelled as the driver's side by the fitting guide itself
(magenta arrow + caption "Handbrake Decal, installed internally to face the driver" pointing at the
driver's door mirror). That same photo's background (a venetian-blind-style reflection pattern visible
in the rear quarter window, plus the door-handle/mirror silhouette) was matched pixel-for-pixel against
p3's top-left "100mm" photo, confirming they are the same source photo of the same side — so p3 top-left
= D1 (driver) with certainty. p3 top-right ("420mm") and p2 bottom-left are therefore the passenger side
(P1) by elimination and by their distinct studio background (no blind-reflection pattern, different
mirror shape visible). Not copied/assumed from the opposite side or another vehicle.

ITEMS SKIPPED / UNVERIFIABLE

W1 — Internal windscreen 'Parking Brake' decal: SKIPPED, unverifiable in a photo.
  p2 contains a magenta arrow running from the caption "Handbrake Decal, installed internally to face
  the driver" to a point on the vehicle photo. That arrow endpoint was cropped and zoomed to full
  resolution (see crops/rav4_w1_zoom.png): it lands on the dark exterior wing-mirror housing / A-pillar
  trim, NOT on glass, and no decal is visible there. The accompanying yellow "ATTENTION / APPLY PARKING
  BRAKE" graphic is separate reference artwork, not a photographed in-place decal. No other photo on
  p1-p3 shows the interior of the windscreen. Left untagged rather than guessed, consistent with the
  same finding/approach used for this same decal type on other vehicles in this project (e.g.
  ranger_single_cab_tray).

W2 — Internal windscreen 'GPS' decal: SKIPPED, unverifiable in a photo.
  Same p2 caption area shows only a disconnected reference-artwork thumbnail (a dark blue rectangle with
  white text/line pattern) labelled "GPS Decal, installed internally on windscreen to face the driver" —
  not a photo of the decal applied to the vehicle. The front-view photo (p2 top-right) does show a
  lighter geometric pattern in the windscreen glass behind the rear-view mirror, but the SAME diagonal
  striped reflection pattern also appears in unrelated side windows throughout p2/p3 (e.g. the rear
  quarter window in the driver-side photo), indicating it is a studio-environment reflection (blinds/
  shelving), not decal artwork — confirmed by cross-checking multiple windows. Left untagged rather than
  risk a false-positive placement.
